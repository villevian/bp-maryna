Vocabulary Jobs and tasks

2ARead about two jobs. Choose a job from Exercise 1A to complete the descriptions.

A I’m a(n) . I’m responsible for a team of five people: two developers, a designer, a tester and a data analyst. We often have meetings with users on Zoom. We sometimes work in the office and we call customers on video calls. At the end of the sprint, we do research to find new ideas for the app. I sometimes go to meetings with important clients, so I often travel for work to our office in Warsaw. I analyse app data, and I often write reports. I usually make calls to the other managers and to my boss, who works in the USA. My working hours are 10 a.m. to 7 p.m., but I’m so busy I never finish work before 7 p.m.

B Olena is a(n) . She usually starts work at 9 o’clock and she always has a lot of work to do. She answers messages in Slack and makes calls or writes emails. She works with the developers and often processes tickets in Jira for them. She sometimes goes to meetings and takes notes. She rarely travels for work.

💡 Product Manager = what we build and why. Project Manager = how and when we build it: tasks, deadlines, people.

Communicative grammar
FACTS AND ROUTINES
+I am/’m a Product Manager.
You/We/They are/’re Developers.		He/She is/’s a UX Designer.
+I/You/We/They always start work at 10 o’clock.
I/You/We/They usually come to the office on Mondays.		He/She often has video calls.
It (the meeting) usually finishes at 11.
−I am not/’m not a Data Analyst.
I/You/We/They do not/don’t write code.		He/She is not/isn’t a Developer.
He/She does not/doesn’t call customers.

he/she/it + verb-s: works, tests, analyses · have → has · go → goes · do → does · Adverb before the verb: I often start… but after be: I’m always busy.
